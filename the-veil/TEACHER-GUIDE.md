# The Veil: teacher guide (Chapter 1, "The Keeper's Bell")

**What it is.** A short investigation game (about 25 to 35 minutes) followed by a writing project. Students create an investigator, explore a closed parish chapel in two worlds (the **Living World** and the **Echo** of one night long ago), solve a bell puzzle and a symbols puzzle, decide what the bell was for, and then write an original horror story.

**Game material.** All places, people and events are fiction ("Game material"). Nothing in this chapter is presented as real history. The supernatural is **observed and investigated, never contacted**: no seances, no Ouija, no spirit-talking. The Echo is a replay the student reads like evidence.

## Six versions, assigned at random
Each case is a different chapel and a different night, with its own clue wording and its own answer to "what was the bell for?" (guide the lost, warn of danger, or gather everyone in). Names change the puzzle answers too (bell pattern, pew-symbol order), so neighbors cannot copy each other. The game assigns each student one of the six cases at random the first time they begin on a device, and keeps it. Students do not choose their case and are not shown which one they have, so two neighbors can get the same case by chance. Leaders can preview any case from the leader tools.

## What students do
1. Find the Keeper's Bell near the road (the bell lets them shift worlds).
2. Write **Field note 1** (10+ words, three senses).
3. Press **V** (or the Veil button) to shift. The gate opens in the Echo and a bell tolls a four-note pattern. Every sound has a caption and a direction cue.
4. Strike the same pattern on the **bell plates** by the chapel doors.
5. Inside, read the **pew marks** in the Echo; in the Living World set the **cabinet dials** front pew to back pew.
6. In the Echo, enter the sacristy and watch the scene. Write **Field note 2** (about 40 words, no feeling words: show, don't tell).
7. Open the **Case File**: choose the best explanation, cite exactly two clues that show it, and explain in 25+ words.
8. **Story Workshop:** nine beats (at least 15 words each, 150 in all). The finished story can be copied or downloaded as a text file.

## Assessment ideas
- Field notes: sensory detail and "show, don't tell".
- Case File: is the claim supported by two documents or scenes? (The game checks this; discuss why the decoy clues do not.)
- Story: use the Haunting Files rubric (atmosphere, clues and foreshadowing, tension, resolution).

## Practical
- Works on desktop Edge/Chrome and on touch tablets (on-screen stick and buttons appear automatically; Settings has a four-arrow D-pad option).
- Keys: arrows/WASD move, E or Enter use, V shift, J evidence, Esc menu.
- Saves stay in the browser. **Saves menu** exports a save file and imports one back. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- Accessibility: every sound has a caption and a direction pulse; there is a mute and volume control, a low-intensity mode (no screen fade, calmer cues), and text size settings.
- If a student is stuck, **Menu → Evidence** shows everything found so far, and the objective line above the game always says what to do next.

## Leaders must check
- That the content (chapel names, "Keeper" characters, the night events) is appropriate for your classes.
- Whether you want students to write the story in class or at home.
