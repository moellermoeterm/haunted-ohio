Step 4. Case file: the Golden Lamb

The Golden Lamb in Lebanon, Ohio began as a stagecoach tavern in 1803. The inn’s historian, John Zimkus, says the number one question guests ask is whether it is haunted. He says he has never met a ghost there, but he shares the stories staff and guests have told him. This case file is built from his 2022 article. We eat lunch at the Golden Lamb on Thursday, so read closely.

The Sarah Stubbs timeline

  1. 1803The inn opens as a stagecoach tavern.
  2. 1882Sarah Stubbs, about five, moves in with her mother and baby sister after her father dies at 32. Her uncle runs the hotel.
  3. 1957Sarah dies at 79, after marrying and raising a family. She did not die at the inn.
  4. 2002A well-known skeptic spends the night in Room 2, near Sarah’s old room.
  5. 2008He publishes an account of what the night clerk told him.

The puzzle

Guests report a little girl in the halls. But Sarah grew up, and she lived a long life elsewhere. So who is on the stairs?

One idea from paranormal researchers is the imprint theory: a traumatic event leaves an imprint, and a spirit may return at the age it was when that event happened. Sarah lost her father and her home at five.

An idea is not evidence. Hold on to that for the next part.

Sort the claims: what kind of evidence is each one?

Documented history is a checkable fact. An eyewitness story is someone’s account. A skeptic’s claim is a debunking argument. A theory is an idea used to explain.

Or had he?

The skeptic wrote that the clerk’s confession meant he had “caught another ghost.” The clerk said she sometimes turned the pictures in Sarah’s room to unsettle the housekeepers.

Think it through: that could explain pictures that were turned. Does it explain a boy’s sighting from years earlier, or laughter heard in a hallway? One debunked detail is not the same as a debunked place. It is also not the same as proof that nothing is there.
